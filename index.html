Your yard could be an oasis — or a desert

To a caterpillar or a hungry chickadee, a lawn of turf grass and imported shrubs might as well be sand — nothing there to eat, nowhere to nest. Six habits turn it back into an oasis. Meet PLANTS — a memory aid I made up, not a study. The table below is the part that's rigorously sourced.

    Keystone genera, checked against the local flora

    What belongs here

    A handful of plant genera do most of the ecological work in a yard — the "keystone genera" that feed the most caterpillars and specialist bees. The trouble is, the standard list is usually quoted at the Level I ecoregion, a unit the size of Texas-to-Canada. You want something a lot more specific — your own county. Below, every genus on the list is checked against the regional flora instead, genus by genus. Where the two disagree, the flora wins — and the rejects stay visible rather than being quietly dropped.

    Six genera that carry the argument. The one-line reading under each is mine, not either source’s.

    From the neighbourhood to one lot

    Your yard is one hexagon

    Everything above is argued at the scale of a bird territory — several adjacent lots, one to two hectares. It is the scale at which the ecology works and the scale at which no individual can act. This is the other end of it: the one lot you control, drawn to scale, planted with species screened against the flora above.

    Part three · The ask

    What I need from this room

    I am not proposing a new organization. NPSOT, BPTMN, Pollinator Pathway and Homegrown National Park already exist and already do the organizing. I am asking three questions.

    ASK 01

    Red-line the screen

    Every row carries an FNCT page number and, where I overrode a source, a sentence saying the call was mine. Those are where I am least sure. I would rather be corrected here than in front of a city arborist.

    ASK 02

    Does a chapter yard registry already exist?

    If members have been logging conversions — NLCP graduates, Wildscapes certifications, anything — that list is worth more than any model I can build. The targeting argument is only useful with real points on it.

    ASK 03

    Is there appetite for a coordinated effort at all?

    Part one argues that adjacency beats linear connection. That is untested here. Before anyone builds a participant-facing anything, I would like to know whether this chapter thinks the question is worth answering.
