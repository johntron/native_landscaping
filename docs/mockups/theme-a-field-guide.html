I

Geometry

Fig. 1 – Fig. 3

A Blackland Prairie songbird territory runs roughly one to two hectares. A quarter-acre yard converted to native planting is a fraction of that — useful to the yard, close to irrelevant to the bird. The question this schematic answers: at what density, and in what arrangement, does a cluster of yards start to read as habitat rather than as scattered ornament?

III

The ask

Appendix A

Three things I don't know and can't resolve from a flora and a spreadsheet.

ASK 01

Red-line the screen

The genus table in Part Two is a first pass against one flora and two databases. If a row is wrong, mark it wrong — that's the point of putting page numbers next to it instead of just a plant list.

ASK 02

Does a chapter yard registry already exist?

If some version of "who's already doing this nearby" is already tracked by a native plant society chapter or a Master Naturalist group, this whole page is redundant and I'd rather point at that than rebuild it.

ASK 03

Is there appetite for a coordinated effort at all?

The geometry in Part One only pays off if yards go in near each other on purpose. That's a coordination problem before it's a planting problem, and I don't know if anyone wants to solve it.

Where I have made a judgement call rather than quoted a source, the row says so and says it is mine. I expect some of it to be wrong. Telling me which parts is the entire point of showing it to you. The assumption I'd most like challenged
