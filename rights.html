Your rights

What a Texas homeowners' association can and cannot make you do about drought-resistant landscaping and turf. Quoted from the statute, not paraphrased.

Statutory information, not legal advice

Texas Property Code Chapter 202

Current through the 89th Legislature, 2nd Called Session (2025). Verified against statutes.capitol.texas.gov on 2026-09-16. This chapter has been amended repeatedly (2003, 2011, 2013, 2021, 2023, 2025) — re-check the primary text before relying on it, and remember city and HOA rules vary on top of it.

No fine for a brown lawn during a watering restriction

Sec. 202.008, added 2025 (H.B. 517, effective 9/1/2025): a property owners' association may not fine an owner because the owner's lawn is brown, dry, or otherwise discolored as a result of a drought or water restriction, or during the 60 days after a watering restriction affecting the property is lifted. This is new law and it is concrete: if your grass is brown because the city told you to water less, a fine for that is against the statute.

An HOA cannot ban drought-resistant landscaping

Sec. 202.007(a)(4) — a property owners' association may not include or enforce a provision that "prohibits or restricts, or has the effect of prohibiting or restricting, a property owner from ... using drought-resistant landscaping or water-conserving natural turf."

Sec. 202.007(d-1) — an association "may not unreasonably deny or withhold approval" of proposed drought-resistant landscaping "or unreasonably determine that the proposed installation is aesthetically incompatible" with the rest of the development.

The same subsection (a) also protects: (a)(1) composting yard vegetation — grass clippings, leaves, brush — including simply leaving grass clippings uncollected; (a)(2) rain barrels and rainwater harvesting systems; and (a)(3) efficient or drip irrigation. Under (b), any provision that violates these is void.

The limits — do not oversell this. The same section that protects you also lets an association regulate around the edges:
  • (c) — an association may restrict the type of turf used in new plantings, to encourage water-conserving turf.
  • (d)(4) — an association may regulate "the installation or use of gravel, rocks, or cacti." Xeriscape hardscape is not protected the way plants are.
  • (d)(5) — an association may regulate yard and landscape maintenance, as long as that regulation does not restrict water-conserving design.
  • (d)(8) — an association may require a detailed description or plan for a proposed installation, submitted for review and approval, to ensure "to the extent practicable" maximum aesthetic compatibility with the development.
  • (e) — Section 202.007 does not apply to an association in a municipality with a population over 175,000 located in a county containing a municipality with a population over 1,000,000, if the association manages 4,000 or more acres restricted to commercial, multifamily, or open-space use. Whether this carve-out reaches a given association takes checking against that association's own location and acreage — it is not a blanket exemption for any particular city.

This page states what the statute says. It is not legal advice, and it does not cover municipal ordinances, which vary by city and are not addressed here. If a dispute is serious enough to matter, read the current statute yourself at statutes.capitol.texas.gov or talk to an attorney.
